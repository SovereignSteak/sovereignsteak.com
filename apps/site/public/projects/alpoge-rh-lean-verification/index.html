Lean verification report

Riemann Hypothesis.

The Riemann Hypothesis says that every nontrivial zero of the zeta function has real part ½. Alpöge’s Lean submission formalizes this statement and related claims for Dirichlet L-functions.

Verification status

On 20 November 2026 at 19:45 UTC, Andrew Sutherland reported that all 175,187 modules compiled and passed Lean kernel replay. A second proof checker, nanoda, accepted the full 61.57 GB proof export. The end-to-end Comparator run was still in progress. Sutherland’s live verification record.

Checking the theorem statements

The first theorem uses Mathlib’s own definition of the Riemann Hypothesis. A separate local review checked the source’s provenance and confirmed that Lean treats each of the four other submitted predicates as definitionally equal to its independently written counterpart. Mathlib’s definition · Alpöge’s paper (PDF).

What the proof checks establish

Sutherland’s audit traced all five theorems to three standard Lean axioms, propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound. The kernel checks give evidence that the formal statements follow from those axioms under Mathlib’s definitions. Axiom audit · Independent kernel log.

Sutherland built and exported the proof outside a sandbox. At the time of his update, a hash comparison with a sandboxed build and checks of the full export by Lean’s default and paranoid kernels were still open. Deicyde’s independent verification setup.
